# Pip Decks Foundations for MBD

*The working principles behind our communication system, distilled from the Pip Decks family — Storyteller Tactics (Steve Rawling), Workshop Tactics (Charles Burdett), Team Tactics and Productivity Tactics. The full card library lives on the NAS: `smb://10.0.0.12/homes/admin/Motion By Design/PipDecks`.*

Pip Decks' core philosophy: **don't rely on talent or memory — use a repeatable tactic for each situation.** A tactic is a small recipe: when to use it, the steps, and what you get out. This doc maps the tactics that matter most to how MBD briefs, gives feedback, and runs projects.

---

## 1. Briefing — "Know the change you want to make"

Storyteller Tactics is built on one idea: a story (or a brief, or a deliverable) exists to **move an audience from A to B**. If you can't name the audience and the change, you don't have a brief yet — you have a task dump.

**Tactics we borrow:**

- **Hero & Guide** — the client (or their customer) is the hero; we are the guide. Briefs and proposals should be framed around *their* outcome, not our deliverable. "Symons Clark wins the Aurizon tender" is a brief; "make an A5 brochure" is a spec line inside it.
- **Movie Time** — describe the scene where the work gets used. *Who is holding this brochure, where, and what do we want them to do 10 seconds later?* One sentence of "movie" removes ten rounds of guessing.
- **Story Hooks** — every deliverable needs one core message. If the brief lists six messages, the tactic is to force-rank them before design starts.

**The rule this produces:** no design work starts until the brief answers **Goal, Audience, Message, Deliverable, Deadline, References, Owner** (see `03-templates/brief.md`). A brief that arrives drip-fed across five Slack messages gets consolidated into the template *before* it's assigned — by whoever received it.

## 2. Feedback — critique the work against the goal, not against taste

Workshop Tactics treats critique as a structured activity, not a reply-thread free-for-all.

**Tactics we borrow:**

- **Rose / Thorn / Bud** — feedback names what works (rose), what doesn't (thorn), and what has potential (bud). Forces balance and stops "just the fixes" feedback that demoralises and loses good elements in V2.
- **Note & Vote** — when several people have opinions, collect them silently first, then converge. For us: *one consolidated feedback pass per round*, not four people dripping comments over two days while the designer has already started V2.
- **Playback** — before acting on feedback (especially client feedback), restate it: "So the change is X because Y — confirmed?" Ambiguous feedback executed literally is the #1 source of wasted rounds.

**The rules this produces:**

1. Feedback is **consolidated** — one person owns collecting all internal/client comments into a single list per round.
2. Feedback is **anchored** — every comment points at a page/timestamp/element, and says *why* (which brief goal it serves), not just *what*.
3. Feedback is **bounded** — the brief states the number of revision rounds; round 2 feedback that contradicts round 1 decisions gets flagged, not silently absorbed.

## 3. Actions & decisions — Who / What / When

The single most-used Workshop Tactics closer: no meeting, thread, or feedback round ends without every action having a **who, a what, and a when**. "Can anyone help with this?" is the anti-pattern — it assigns the work to nobody.

**The rules this produces:**

1. Every request names **one owner** (a person, not a channel).
2. Every request carries a **real deadline** ("EOD Thursday ACST", not "when you get a chance").
3. Decisions get written down where the project lives, not buried in a thread. A one-line decision log per project channel (pinned canvas) beats perfect memory.

---

## 4. Project rhythm — priorities visible, retros regular

- **Priority Map** (Workshop Tactics) — plot work by importance vs urgency *together*, weekly. For us: Monday priorities post per person — what's shipping this week, what's blocked.
- **Sailboat Retrospective** — monthly, 30 minutes: what's the wind (helping us), what's the anchor (dragging us), what are the rocks ahead (risks). This is where communication problems get fixed instead of repeated.

---

## 5. Client communication — guide, don't just respond

- **Pitch Perfect / Simple Sales Stories** — proposals and recommendations follow problem → stakes → guide → plan → outcome. We recommend; we don't just quote.
- **Hero & Guide** again — status emails lead with *the client's* progress ("You're on track for the conference"), then our asks.
- **Playback after every call** — a 5-line recap email (decisions, actions, who/what/when) within 24h of any client meeting. The recap *is* the record; it prevents "that's not what we agreed" three weeks later.
