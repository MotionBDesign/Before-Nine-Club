# Client Email Audit — Feb–Aug 2026 (mostly Jun–Aug)

*Method: ~19 substantive threads read in full across Aurizn, ResMed, SA Power Networks, Symons Clark, Fujifilm Instax, Brighter/CCA, Law Society SA, Discovery Parks, CMAX, Ilim College/MMS, plus print suppliers.*

## Andrea — the account manager

**Style:** the most "AM-shaped" communicator: plays briefs back, clarifies with screenshots, sequences deliverables, works print deadlines backwards. Warm to the point of informality ("Heya" to C-level).

**At her best:**
- "Thanks for sending this brief through, it's really clear… Just one question regarding recording the presentations. To capture high-quality audio… we'll need access to the event's audio feed" (Aurizn Leadership Breakfast — brief playback + technical risk raised early).
- "Were you after filming just the breakfast event, or would you also like us to capture: content at the booth / candid team shots / quick interviews…" (proactive scope menu).
- "Would you like us to completely remove the 'Get the perfect scan' messaging, or simply move it lower in the hierarchy?" (binary clarifying question — unambiguous answer in 5 minutes).

**Weak spots:**
- **Client-caught missed edits, three times in a fortnight** ("There are just three edits that were missed in the last round"; "there was an update missed from the updates I sent through last time" → "Apologies, we misread the content on our end!") — each cost a round-trip on deadline-critical print jobs.
- **10-day silence on the biggest brief of the month** (ADSTAR): client chased ("Is this something you can support?"); reply had sat "stuck in my outbox".
- No cost/scope confirmation at kickoff (Quad Chart accepted with no quote, no ETA, submission deadline the next day after first draft).

## Yami — the engine room

**Style:** fast, high-volume, deadline-driven. The team's best approval-chaser and work-stager (script → storyboard → animation, styleframes before rollout).

**At her best:**
- "Once you have reviewed and approved these files… we will roll out the new assets. I suggest reviewing the disclaimers in the spreadsheet… to prevent back-and-forth adjustments" (approval gate + friction prevention).
- Caught a client data error before build: "Could you please clarify if the promo listed as 'AS 10' is meant to be the AirMini bundle?" → "yes apologies".
- Deadline-anchored chasing: "I have allocated the team to export them, so I would like to follow up on the approvals… to meet the deadline by the end of the day" — delivered same day.
- Options with trade-offs: "we do not have footage demonstrating the incorrect examples. Two options: 1. Return to site… 2. Animate the scenes…"

**Weak spots:**
- **Vague ETAs**: "soon", "nearly there", "end of this week or sooner" — concrete dates only appear when the client sets them first.
- **Register whiplash**: "Heyaaa team!", "Tks heaps!", "you're a legend!", emoji-only replies — fine with some contacts, risky as the default with corporates (SA Power Networks).
- **Internal ops leaked to clients**: told Symons Clark twice that work was waiting on internal priority decisions ("we haven't heard back from Jason regarding our priorities") — reads as "your project isn't the priority".
- Terse deliveries with no change list ("Please find a revised version attached") — pushes verification onto the client.

## Dom — the rainmaker

**Style:** warm, matey, commercially the sharpest (budget-first framing, retainer visibility, goal-setting meetings). Best emails on the team when he takes time; sloppiest day-to-day mechanics.

**At his best:**
- Law Society quote update: "the quote is almost ready… we're just waiting on one final estimate from a second talent agency. As soon as that lands we'll finalise the numbers" (unprompted interim update; quote next morning with line-item rationale).
- Symons Clark "workload" email: structured status/priority list per stream — model comms (undersold by the subject line and a "runt through" typo).
- Budget-first scoping: "it might be worth throwing a figure at this for us to work backwards from… we will make it as fancy/amazing as possible according to the budget" → $700 confirmed before work started.
- Commercial transparency: flagged unused paid scope to ResMed ("Two paid projects are still pending kickoff…").

**Weak spots:**
- **Typos in most emails**: "breifed", "might of", "runt through", "You're are doing", "particulary" — noticeable at MD level.
- **Delivery mechanics fail silently**: wrong Drive link twice ("Gosh, sorry! Third time's the charm"), a Xero quote that never arrived, own domain spam-foldering with two clients — all discovered by the client.
- Bare no-text forwards; implicit asks ("Let me know if you have any deadlines" when the client had already implied the date).

## Cross-cutting: top problems

1. **Clients are the QA function** — four client-caught missed-edit rounds in one month. No "changes made: 1/2/3" playback accompanies deliveries.
2. **Deadline-responsive but not deadline-declarative** — vague ETAs unless the client sets a date.
3. **No scope/cost gate at kickoff** for most jobs (Law Society/CCA the exceptions).
4. **Silent delivery failures** — wrong links, lost Xero quote, mutual spam-foldering; no "can you confirm this opens?" habit.
5. **Uncalibrated informality** as the default register regardless of audience.
6. **Internal operations leaking** into client threads.
7. **Reactive follow-up** — clients initiate the status check too often (10-day ADSTAR silence; "how are the next cuts coming along?").
8. **No change summary / final-version record on delivery** — "print-ready" wasn't a gated state; brochure needed an overnight file swap at the printer.

## Cross-cutting: top strengths

1. Turnaround speed clients repeatedly praise unprompted.
2. Genuine clarifying questions before execution (AS10 catch, remove-vs-demote, audio feed).
3. Staged approval gates (Yami especially).
4. Deadline-anchored approval chasing that converts deadlines into client action.
5. Warm relationships that keep expanding scope — goodwill is a real asset, currently being spent covering process gaps.

## Rework/delay incidents (the cost of the gaps)

- **Aurizn ADSTAR**: 10-day unsent reply + two missed-edit rounds → print deadline compressed to "artwork must go tomorrow".
- **Aurizn Quad Chart**: missed edits twice → final approval landed the morning of the submission deadline.
- **Brighter/CCA Gala**: spam-foldering (~5 days) + wrong link twice → ~1 week lost on a 3-week job; client screenshot-proved the link was wrong.
- **ResMed quote**: Xero delivery never arrived; found only because the client flagged it.
- **Symons Clark brochure**: "print-ready" file failed on the print test after the printer started; saved by an overnight swap and the printer hand-delivering.
- **Sodium Chlorate**: ~1 week of ambiguous status while internal priorities were visibly unresolved in front of the client.
