The idea everything else follows from

Imagery frees colour

In round one I refused to give twelve clients twelve colours, because that burns the one channel that should scream. That rule was right, but I paid for it with a grey interface — with no imagery, the only way to tell a Resmed job from a SAPN job was to read.

Thumbnails fix both problems at once. The artwork identifies the client instantly and better than any swatch could, which means colour is still free to mean nothing but "this needs you". You get a far more visual dashboard and a stricter use of colour, from the same move.

And it is not decoration. You are a motion studio: your jobs already have a picture attached. Showing it is the fastest possible label.

The screen

Glass, where it earns its place

Apple's current material language is translucency and depth — real refraction over live content, not a grey panel with opacity turned down. It is genuinely useful in exactly one spot here: a floating layer that sits over the work without hiding it. Everywhere else it costs legibility for nothing.

What to do with this

The look is three changes deep

You do not have to rebuild anything to get most of this. The visual jump from round one to here is carried by three decisions, and the rest is polish that follows on its own.

1

Put a picture on every job

The ClickUp cover image, the newest Drive export, or a frame from the edit. This is the whole visual difference, and it is a data-plumbing job rather than a design one.

2

Lift the type and the spacing

One friendly sans, weight 650–800 for anything that matters, negative tracking on big numbers, and roughly double the padding you have now. Costs nothing, changes everything.

3

Lead with a sentence

Every screen opens with plain English — "Two clients are waiting on us." Numbers support it underneath. This is the single biggest clarity win and it needs no new data at all.

Still just mockups. Nothing here has been built into :3100 or :3200, and I have no route to them from this session. These are pictures of what your data could look like, for you to react to.

The structural thinking — capacity model, allocation flow, notification tiers, build order — is unchanged and still lives in round one. This round replaces how it looks, not what it does.

Two things would sharpen this further: a screenshot of the Bonus tab, so this matches a look you already like rather than one I picked; and confirmation of whether ClickUp cover images exist on enough of your tasks to carry the thumbnails, or whether they need to come from Drive instead.
