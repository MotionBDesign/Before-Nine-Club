# MBD Client Email

Structure and tone for client email, built on Hero & Guide (client's outcome first), BLUF (lead with the point), and Who/What/When actions.

## Structure (any email type)

1. **One-line why**: the first sentence states what this email is for ("Recap and next steps from today's call", "V2 attached — two things to confirm").
2. **The client's progress**: frame status around their outcome ("You're on track for the conference") before our asks.
3. **Asks as a list**, each with owner + date. If a client action is on the critical path, state the downstream effect: "We need photos by Tue to hold the print date."
4. **Scope and money in writing, at the moment they change** — not at invoice time. One neutral sentence: "That addition sits outside the current quote; happy to include for $X / in round 3."
5. **Close with the single next event** (date of next version, call, or deadline).

## For specific types
- **Meeting recap** (within 24h of any call): use `docs/comms/03-templates/meeting-recap-email.md`.
- **Delivering revisions — ALWAYS include Delivery Playback**: a numbered "Changes made" list mirroring the client's feedback items, plus anything NOT done and why. Never send "please find the revised version attached" bare — the Aug 2026 audit found four client-caught missed-edit rounds in one month, all traceable to deliveries without a change list.
- **Delivering work for feedback**: state the round number, what kind of feedback is useful at this stage ("layout and messaging, not final copy"), the feedback deadline, and where to put comments (one consolidated reply / annotated PDF).
- **Chasing**: reference the dependency, restate the consequence, give an easy out ("If Thursday's tricky, tomorrow we can still make it work by moving X").

## Delivery mechanics (silent failures are the enemy)
- Anything that matters (links, quotes, print files) ends with a landing check: "Can you confirm the link opens for you?" Quotes sent via Xero or other systems get a direct email note too.
- ETAs are dates and times, never "soon" / "nearly there" / "end of the week or sooner". Set the date before the client asks.
- Internal operations stay internal — never "we're waiting to hear from [colleague] about priorities". Externally: "We'll confirm the schedule by [date]."
- Print jobs: "print-ready" only after an internal pre-flight proof; say which check was done.

## Tone rules
- Warm, direct, Australian-professional. One pleasantry line max before the point. Calibrate register to the reader — long-standing friendly contact ≠ C-suite/corporate default; "Heya"/emoji-only replies are opt-in, not the house style.
- Proofread pass is mandatory: flag typos and grammar slips in any draft you review (the audit found them in most outgoing emails).
- No over-apologising, no hedging ("just", "sorry to bother", "whenever you get a chance" are banned on critical-path asks).
- Confirm receipt of client requests same-day with an ETA, even if the work comes later.
- Subject lines carry the project and the action: "[SC Mining Brochure] V2 for review — feedback by Thu".
